# Trinity v2.4 — Plan

## TL;DR

The 2026-08-21 doc-bot build retrospective mined every converged trinity loop in that repo — 3 `iterate-plan` convergences (23 passes) and 19 `iterate-review` convergences (108 passes) — and found that **fold-chaining, not reviewer thoroughness, drove pass counts**: 29% of real application-code defects (~48 of ~166) existed only because a previous fold created them; reviews with ≥6 fold-caused findings averaged 10.8 passes against 3.3 for reviews with ≤1. The stall guardrail never once fired correctly, and the one signal that predicted "stop" in every long loop — *was this finding created by the previous fold?* — was hand-recorded in every pass log and aggregated by nothing.

v2.4 turns those findings into machinery in two phases. **Phase 0** ships five small, individually-evidenced fixes: register seeding at plan time becomes non-optional in `create-plan`; both loop skills gain a fold-time hygiene checklist targeting the two named preventable defect species; `iterate-review` gets a scope classifier that shrinks the loop budget for diffs that touch no production code; `iterate-plan` freezes open-question answers that have stopped changing; and both skills gain fold-provenance instrumentation (`introduced_by_pass`) — the data-collection half of the punted stop-machinery decision ([issue #6](https://github.com/kaileconsulting/trinity-skills/issues/6)), with **no stop behavior shipped**. **Phase 1** ports v2.3.0's proportionality machinery into `iterate-plan` — the `accepted-risk` disposition and lifecycle, register-aware lens inputs, and the decision-card contract — which is the explicit revisit the v2.3.0 archive promised and never got.

Expected outcome: fewer fold-induced passes in both loops, a plan loop that can decline as well as fold, and per-pass provenance data that makes the next retrospective a `jq` one-liner instead of two mining agents — and gives issue #6's build/no-build decision an actual evidence base.

## Why / Context

**This plan is the revisit v2.3.0 deferred and never took up.** The v2.3.0 plan (archived at `docs/archive/risk-posture-proportionality-2026-08-12.md`) scoped proportionality machinery to `iterate-review` only, with the out-of-scope rationale "plan review hasn't shown the same churn — revisit after Phase 3 evidence" (2026-08-12). That rationale was falsified within 48 hours: the runtime-pipeline plan ran 12 `iterate-plan` passes, the architect lens returned a HIGH on 12 of 12 because every HIGH was structurally mandatory to fold, and passes 9–12 were four consecutive, 100% fold-induced passes producing acceptance-coverage redistribution and vocabulary hygiene — not design. The "revisit after Phase 3 evidence" note was never revisited. This plan says so and is that revisit.

The rest of the evidence base comes from the 2026-08-21 doc-bot retrospective (doc-bot `docs/reviews/retro-2026-08-21.md`, [PR #21](https://github.com/Adminformatics/doc-bot/pull/21) — private repo; the numbers below are self-contained aggregates per the public-repo evidence policy):

- **Fold-chaining drove pass counts, not reviewer thoroughness.** 29% of real application-code defects (~48 of ~166) existed only because a previous fold created them. Reviews with ≥6 fold-caused findings averaged **10.8 passes**; reviews with ≤1 averaged **3.3**. Two named preventable sub-species:
  - *Claims-vs-behavior debris* (~25–30 findings): a correct fix leaving its own description lying — test name, docstring, log line, comment — "none of which any passing test could have caught."
  - *The un-walked global invariant* (plan-side): a rule folded into one section without walking it through every other section that restates it — self-diagnosed 4× in one plan.
- **The H+M-count stall guardrail never once correctly identified a stall.** 0-for-2 in code reviews, both rightly overridden by the human; it would have fired 5 more times, indistinguishably, on the 12-pass plan. The signal that actually predicted "stop" — fold provenance — was hand-recorded in prose in every long loop's pass log and aggregated by nothing.
- **An unseeded register cost five re-litigations of one settled decision.** doc-bot's Q3 auth-posture position was re-argued across write-path phases 0, 1, and 3, and twice more in one later review. Because `iterate-review`'s provenance gate honors only register entries predating the diff under review, late seeding is unrecoverable — plan time is the last cheap moment, making the seeding prompt the highest-leverage small fix in this plan.
- **The machinery works where it exists.** Disputes and accepted-risks held at ~94%: of ~18, exactly one was ever re-raised — and that one correctly (it was falsified and folded). The proportionality machinery earns its keep in `iterate-review`; it simply doesn't exist in `iterate-plan`.
- **Test-only diffs produced zero production bugs in 10 passes** across 4 reviews; one pass re-reviewed a byte-identical diff purely to satisfy the convergence gate.
- **Open-question channel churn:** Q1–Q4 on one plan were answered identically 12 consecutive times, at full lens-input cost each time. Precedent for the fix: dropping the PM lens after APPROVE ×3 halved fan-out at zero cost.
- **The retro itself cost two mining agents** because all of the above lived in prose. Instrumentation makes the next one a `jq` one-liner.

## Who / Use cases

- **Kyle (operator, checkpoint human)** — fewer passes spent watching the loop chase its own folds; gets a checkpoint observation when a loop turns fully self-referential instead of silent churn; the next retrospective costs a query, not two agents.
- **The editor (Claude, folding findings)** — a two-item hygiene check at fold time that targets the two named debris species; in `iterate-plan`, the same decline vocabulary (`accepted-risk`, pushback criteria, decision cards) it already has in `iterate-review`, instead of a loop where every HIGH is structurally mandatory to fold.
- **The reviewer (Codex lenses)** — in plan review, sees the repo's accepted-risks register so settled decisions stop being re-litigated; stops re-answering frozen open questions at full input cost.
- **Future plan authors** — every scaffold now explicitly asks whether known risks are being accepted, so the register is seeded when it's still cheap and the provenance gate can honor it.
- **Issue #6's future decision review** — inherits structured `introduced_by_pass` data from every review run after Phase 0, instead of prose archaeology.

## Risk posture

### Full variant (initiatives)

#### PF-audience — Audience & reach

Kyle, as the single operator, plus every Claude/Codex session running the trinity loops across his repos. The repo is public, but external readers are passive consumers — nothing in these skills executes for them.

#### PF-blast — Blast radius & recoverability

A defective skill change degrades the review process itself, and the worst failure mode is *silent* — a loop that converges early or suppresses a real finding looks identical to a healthy one from outside. The skill files revert trivially via git; the real cost is un-reviewed defects reaching downstream builds before anyone notices. Phase 0's loop instrumentation is itself the detection mechanism for this class.

#### PF-shipbar — Ship bar

Any change that could cause a loop to terminate early or suppress a finding **without a human decision point** blocks ship — the convergence gate and the human-confirm steps are the trust boundary, and full rigor applies there regardless of posture. Ergonomics, prompt wording, instrumentation, and pass-budget tuning can ship with logged accepted risks.

## Goals (MVP)

- `create-plan` Step 3 asks about accepted risks on every scaffold, both plan types — the seeding path no longer depends on the author volunteering one.
- Both loop skills carry a fold-time hygiene checklist (claims-vs-behavior sweep; plan-side invariant walk), pinned as shared rules by `tools/check-parity.py`.
- `iterate-review` classifies each diff production / non-production at setup; non-production diffs get a reduced loop-mode pass-budget default, always human-overridable, with the classification recorded in the pass log header.
- `iterate-plan` freezes open-question answers after N identical consecutive passes; a frozen question reopens when an edit touches it or a lens brings genuinely new evidence.
- Both skills record `introduced_by_pass` per finding and a per-pass summary (pass, verdict, H+M count, provenance tally, disposition counts) in the existing state files — the data half of issue #6, with no stop machinery.
- `iterate-plan` gains the v2.3.0 proportionality machinery: `accepted-risk` disposition with the full lifecycle and accounting table, register-aware lens inputs, pushback criteria/anti-criteria, and the decision-card contract — kept in semantic parity with `iterate-review` by `check-parity.py`.
- Phase 1's success is measured by **plan-loop pass counts and re-litigation counts** on future plans — explicitly *not* by code-review pass counts, which the retro shows are driven by fold-chaining (Phase 0 items 2/5 and, eventually, issue #6), not by plan quality.

## Non-goals (MVP)

- **No fold-provenance stop machinery.** Punted by explicit decision to issue #6, data-gated on Phase 0's instrumentation (see Out of scope).
- **No convergence-math changes.** The scope classifier tunes a *default budget*; the stall guardrail, worst-of verdicts, and Converge predicates are untouched.
- **No lens-selection changes.** The APPROVE×3 lens-dropping episode is cited as precedent for question-freezing, not generalized into selection rules.
- **No live-validation phase.** v2.3.0's still-open "live review against a real posture file" acceptance closes naturally on doc-bot's next phase; this plan doesn't re-scope it (see Out of scope).
- **No retro-editing of existing pass logs.** Instrumentation is forward-only; no backfilled provenance data.

## Approach

The five Phase 0 items are independent, individually small, and each carries its own evidence. Phase 1 is a port, not an invention — the lifecycle, accounting table, and card format transfer from `iterate-review` nearly verbatim, which is exactly what the parity machinery exists to keep honest.

### §1 Non-optional register seeding (`create-plan`)

`create-plan/SKILL.md` Step 3 currently seeds the accepted-risks register only "**if the author dictates an existing accepted risk**" while answering the three posture questions — conditional on the author volunteering. Change: Step 3 gains an explicit fourth prompt, asked on every scaffold for both plan types, after the three `PF-` questions:

> Are you already accepting any known risks — specific, real behaviors you've decided not to fix?

"None" proceeds with nothing written (no empty-register scaffolding, no ceremony). A dictated risk flows into the existing seeding procedure (Step 3's numbered sub-steps 1–5: resolve `docs/risk-posture.md`, create/append under `## Accepted risks`, `RR-<date>-<slug>` shape, push back on category-shaped suppressions) — that procedure is unchanged. The author-dictation-is-its-own-confirmation rule is unchanged.

Why this is worth a whole plan item: `iterate-review`'s provenance gate honors only register entries that predate the diff under review, so a register seeded *after* code exists can never wave through the findings it was written for — late seeding is unrecoverable, and the retro paid five re-litigations of one settled decision for it. Plan time is the last cheap moment, and the current prompt shape silently misses any author who doesn't think to volunteer.

### §2 Fold-time hygiene checklist (both loop skills)

`iterate-review` step 12 and `iterate-plan` step 8 gain a short, mandatory per-fold check, applied to each finding before its disposition is written:

1. **Claims-vs-behavior sweep:** did every description of the changed behavior change with it — test name, docstring, log line, comment (and, plan-side, the plan prose that describes the mechanism)? A correct fix that leaves its own description lying seeds the next pass's finding, and no passing test can catch it.
2. **Invariant walk (plan-side):** if this fold states or changes a rule that other sections restate — an id format, a lifecycle, an accounting rule — does the rule now read correctly in *every* section that restates it?

Deliberately two items, not ten: a long checklist gets skimmed, and these two target the named species that account for the bulk of preventable fold-chaining (~25–30 claims-vs-behavior findings; the invariant miss self-diagnosed 4× in one plan). Both items become SHARED MACHINERY rules pinned by `tools/check-parity.py`, so neither skill can drift out of them silently.

### §3 Scope classifier + reduced pass budget (`iterate-review`)

At setup, after step 3 resolves the diff, the editor classifies it:

- **non-production** — every touched path is tests, docs, fixtures, goldens, or examples;
- **production** — anything else, and any ambiguous case (ambiguity biases toward the full budget, mirroring lens selection's bias toward inclusion).

For a non-production diff, loop mode's `--max-passes` **default** drops to a reduced budget (Q2 — recommend 2). An explicit `--max-passes=N` always wins, in either class — this is a default, not a cap, so the human can always buy more passes. The classification is recorded in the pass log header (`Scope class: production | non-production`) so Phase 0's instrumentation can later falsify the heuristic if reduced-budget reviews start missing real bugs.

Classification is editor-side (it's loop control, and the runner never decides); the path heuristic is written into SKILL.md so it's inspectable and consistent. Evidence: test-only diffs produced zero production bugs in 10 passes across 4 reviews, and one pass re-reviewed a byte-identical diff purely to satisfy the convergence gate.

### §4 Open-question freeze (`iterate-plan`)

When a Q-numbered open question has received **identical answers from all lenses, with zero new refinements, for N consecutive passes** (Q1 — recommend N=3, matching the APPROVE×3 lens-dropping precedent), the editor marks it frozen in the plan's `## Open questions` section — an inline annotation naming the freezing pass and the reopen rule, e.g. `— FROZEN at pass 7 (answered identically ×3; reopens on any edit touching this question or new evidence)`. Frozen questions stay visible in the plan; `iterate-plan`'s `reviewer-prompt.md` gains an instruction not to re-answer a frozen question absent genuinely new evidence (a lens that *has* new evidence answers anyway, and that answer reopens the question).

Reopening: any plan edit touching the question's subject matter reopens it (editor judgment at fold time, noted in the HISTORICAL block), as does a lens answer carrying new evidence. Freezing never resolves a question — `needs_human` escalation and the final human answer at Converge are untouched; freezing only stops the loop paying full lens-input cost for the 5th–12th identical restatement.

The reviewer-prompt change regenerates `iterate-plan`'s composition goldens in the same commit (same discipline as v2.3.0 Phase 1). Evidence: Q1–Q4 answered identically 12 consecutive times at full cost.

### §5 Fold-provenance instrumentation (both skills) — the data half of issue #6

Two additions, and explicitly nothing more:

1. **Per-finding field.** Every finding's HISTORICAL entry gains `introduced_by_pass: <N | null>` — `N` when the defect the finding describes was created by an edit a previous pass's fold made (the same judgment call every long loop's pass log already recorded in prose; the editor makes it at fold time, when the causal chain is freshest), `null` when the defect pre-existed the review or arrived with the original diff. When genuinely uncertain, `null` with a one-line note — under-claiming keeps the data conservative.
2. **Per-pass summary.** At Converge/Abort, the existing state file (`state/<scope-hash>.json` in each skill; written at convergence/abort only — that contract is unchanged, see Q4) gains a `passes` array: one row per pass with pass number, verdict, H+M finding count, provenance tally (findings carrying a non-null `introduced_by_pass`), and disposition counts. The rows are derived from the pass log, which remains the durable authority.

A documented one-line `jq` recipe (in the skill READMEs or `docs/`) reproduces the retro's core tallies — pass counts, fold-caused share, disposition mix — across state files. **Ship no stop machinery:** no guardrail, checkpoint, budget, or recommendation consults the new field. That decision stays punted to [issue #6](https://github.com/kaileconsulting/trinity-skills/issues/6), which is data-gated on exactly this instrumentation; the issue gets updated at closeout to point at the shipped field.

### §6 Port proportionality machinery into `iterate-plan` (Phase 1)

All pieces exist in `iterate-review` from v2.3.0; the parity requirement means they transfer nearly verbatim, with three genuine adaptation points:

- **`accepted-risk` disposition + lifecycle.** `iterate-plan` step 8's dispositions (`incorporated | skipped | disputed`) gain `accepted-risk` with the full v2.3.0 lifecycle: stable `AR-<n>` ids minted when the disposition is written into the HISTORICAL block (the plan file is `iterate-plan`'s pass log, so the plan is the durable record and the sole id allocator), `proposed` → `confirmed`/`rejected` (human only), `rejected` → `reopened`, material-change-mints-new-id with the finding-bound vs posture-text disambiguation, posture-dependency descriptors + sha256 digests over `PF-`/`RR-` field ids, and the three-consumer accounting table (HIGH guardrail / H+M non-convergence counter / Converge predicate — `iterate-plan`'s step-10 guardrails have the same three consumers). Pushback criteria/anti-criteria transfer as-is, including the `PF-shipbar` trust-boundary exclusion.
- **Register-aware lens inputs.** Plans already carry their `PF-` fields into every lens input — the full plan, `## Risk posture` included, *is* the pass input — so only the **register** needs plumbing: composition gains the repo's `docs/risk-posture.md` `## Accepted risks` entries (mechanism per Q5 — recommended: a runner composition change, since `iterate-plan`'s composition is wholly runner-owned and reading a named repo file is deterministic composition, not decision-making; goldens regenerate in the same commit). `iterate-plan`'s `reviewer-output.schema.json` gains the optional per-finding `register_ref`; the editor validates matches with the **behavior** and **trust-boundary** gates transferred as-is, and a provenance analog adapted for plan review, where no diff exists (recommended: the entry must exist, with matching digest, in `docs/risk-posture.md` at HEAD — a working-tree-only entry fails the gate, closing the same self-serving-edit hole with the materials plan review actually has).
- **Reviewer-prompt instruction + severity semantics.** `iterate-plan`'s `reviewer-prompt.md` gains the posture/register weighing instruction: severity stays **absolute** (v2.3.0 Q2's settled semantics — worst credible outcome; proportionality lives entirely in disposition), lenses set `register_ref` on independently-actionable matches, and don't re-file findings that merely restate a documented posture or register decision without new evidence. Goldens regenerate.
- **Decision cards.** The shared card contract transfers verbatim — recommendation + why, up to 3 alternatives with context-sensitive omission (floor: recommendation + discuss), two-phase persistence (written `(pending)` before presentation, resolved in place, re-presented on resume), harness 4-option cardinality bound — with `iterate-plan`'s judgment-moment mapping: **accepted-risk confirmation**, **`needs_human` question**, **non-convergence stall**, **malformed posture/register source** (duplicate `RR-` ids, or a plan whose own `## Risk posture` carries partial `PF-` fields), and the plan-review analog of the design-shaped-fold escalation (Q3 — recommended: a *plan-shaping fold*, one that would add/remove a phase, change Goals/Non-goals, or redistribute acceptance coverage across phases, escalates as a card instead of folding silently; that is precisely the passes-9–12 churn class). Cards persist in the plan's HISTORICAL blocks under the same two-phase discipline.
- **Parity.** `tools/check-parity.py`'s shared-rule set extends to cover the lifecycle, accounting table, and card contract, so the two skills can't drift apart on the machinery they now genuinely share.

**Expectation calibration, recorded here so it's measured fairly:** Phase 1's success metric is **plan-loop pass counts and re-litigation counts** on future plans — *not* code-review pass counts. The retro is explicit that code-review churn is driven by fold-chaining, which Phase 0's items 2 and 5 (and, eventually, issue #6) address — not by plan quality. Judging this port by downstream code-review passes would blame it for a variable it doesn't control.

### This plan's own review — hand-piloting issue #6's stop rule

When `iterate-plan` runs on this plan, the editor hand-applies the punted stop rule as a pilot: at each fold, note whether the pass's findings trace to a previous pass's fold (this is exactly the `introduced_by_pass` judgment §5 formalizes), and **if two consecutive passes are fully fold-induced, surface that to Kyle as a checkpoint observation** rather than continuing silently. No machinery, no gate — a free trial of the punted mechanism on the cheapest possible subject: the plan about the mechanism itself. The observations land in the pass HISTORICAL blocks so they're on the record for issue #6.

## Risks

### R1 — "Stop earlier" is supported but not proven
The retro itself flags this: the 12-pass plan's phase reviews then converged unusually fast (40 passes for a larger surface vs. 49 for a comparable one), so late plan passes may buy downstream value that pass-count accounting alone doesn't see. Freezes and reduced budgets could trade cheap plan passes for expensive code passes.
**Mitigation:** everything in this plan that reduces work is a *default with a human override*, never a hard stop; Phase 0's instrumentation is precisely what makes the trade answerable — if the data later shows late passes paying rent, defaults are one-line reverts.

### R2 — The reduced budget is a depth cap, and naive depth caps are wrong
The retro found the marginal-pass hit rate did **not** decline with review depth (survivorship — long reviews were long because they kept finding real things). That is exactly why issue #6's stop machinery was punted, and the scope classifier's reduced budget is a small depth cap.
**Mitigation:** the cap applies only to the one class where the evidence is unambiguous — diffs touching no production code, where 10 passes across 4 reviews found zero production bugs; ambiguity classifies as production; explicit `--max-passes` always wins; the `Scope class:` header field means instrumentation can falsify the heuristic from the data.

### R3 — Checklist and prose bloat dilute compliance
Both SKILL.md files are already long (~430 lines each); every added rule competes for the editor's attention, and a skimmed checklist is worse than none because it *reads* as covered.
**Mitigation:** the checklist is two items, targeting the two named species that account for the bulk of preventable fold-chaining; both are parity-pinned so they can't silently drift; no other Phase 0 item adds recurring per-fold ceremony.

### R4 — Question-freezing suppresses a real late refinement
A frozen question could mute the one pass where a lens finally had something new to say.
**Mitigation:** freezing requires identical answers with *zero* refinements ×N; a lens with genuinely new evidence answers anyway and that answer reopens the question; any edit touching the question reopens it; frozen questions stay visible in the plan and are still resolved by the human at Converge — freezing stops re-asking, never resolves.

### R5 — Provenance judgment produces garbage data
`introduced_by_pass` is an editor judgment call, and a sloppily-assigned field would poison exactly the dataset issue #6's decision depends on.
**Mitigation:** the judgment rule is written into both skills (fold-caused iff the defect was created by an edit a previous pass's fold made — the same call every long loop's pass log already made reliably in prose, per the retro); uncertainty resolves to `null` with a note, biasing the data conservative; the per-pass tally is derived from the pass log, so every value is auditable against its prose.

### R6 — Plan-review proportionality has a weaker case than code-review proportionality
Plans are cheaper to fix than code, so `accepted-risk` in plan review risks declining folds that were nearly free — overcorrecting a loop whose real problem was fold-chaining, not over-folding.
**Mitigation:** the anti-criteria transfer intact (never on trust-boundary content, never to avoid a small honest fix); every acceptance still requires human confirmation to converge; and the expectation-calibration note means the port is judged on re-litigation and plan-loop pass counts, where the retro's evidence (passes 9–12; the 12/12 architect HIGHs) actually lives.

## Rollback plan

All changes are skill prose, schemas, fixtures, and goldens — `git revert` restores any prior state completely; no data migrations, no persisted-state compatibility concerns (new state-file fields are additive; old state files remain readable).

One hazard is live *during* the work, not after it: `~/.claude/skills/*` are symlinks into this repo's checkout, so any in-progress commit on the checked-out branch is immediately live for concurrent loop sessions in other repos — the 2026-08-13 "ALL LENSES FAILED" incident in doc-bot traced to exactly this (a schema mid-fix on the active branch). Mitigation: all v2.4 work happens on a feature branch with main checked out only at merge time, and no concurrent reviews run in downstream repos during the merge window.

## Sequencing decision

Phase 0 before Phase 1, for two reasons beyond size: (1) Phase 0 §5 defines the measurement regime — provenance tallies and per-pass summaries — under which Phase 1's port (and everything after it) gets evaluated, so instrumentation should exist before the machinery it measures; (2) Phase 0's items are independent small fixes that de-risk the shared fold path before Phase 1 adds new fold semantics to `iterate-plan`. The plan's own `iterate-plan` run happens before either phase builds, and hand-pilots issue #6's stop rule at zero machinery cost.

## Open questions

- **Q1.** What is N for the open-question freeze — how many identical consecutive passes before a question freezes? **Recommendation: N=3.** Matches the APPROVE×3 precedent that justified dropping the PM lens, and the retro's worst case (12 identical answers) would have saved 9 rounds at N=3. N=2 risks freezing on coincidence; N=4+ leaves most of the observed waste on the table.
- **Q2.** What is the reduced loop-mode `--max-passes` default for non-production diffs? **Recommendation: 2.** The evidence class averaged 2.5 passes with zero production bugs found in 10; two passes covers "review + verify the fold" and anything genuinely wrong with test/doc changes surfaces by then. Explicit `--max-passes` always overrides, so the cost of guessing low is one flag.
- **Q3.** What is the plan-review analog of `iterate-review`'s design-shaped-fold escalation? **Recommendation: the plan-shaping fold** — a fold that would add/remove a phase, change Goals/Non-goals, or redistribute acceptance coverage across phases halts at that finding with a decision card. That's the passes-9–12 churn class from the retro, and it mirrors the code-side rule's spirit: never silently commit to a structural change nobody approved. Alternative: skip the fifth moment entirely in v2.4 and port only the four that transfer mechanically — cheaper, but leaves the loop with no vocabulary for exactly the churn that falsified the v2.3.0 deferral.
- **Q4.** Where does the per-pass summary land — incremental per-pass writes to the state file, or aggregation at Converge/Abort from the pass log? **Recommendation: aggregate at Converge/Abort.** It preserves both skills' existing "state is written at convergence/abort only" contract, keeps the pass log the single durable authority, and loses nothing: an interrupted review still has its pass log, from which the rows derive. Incremental writes buy crash-completeness for the state file at the cost of a new mid-loop write path — not worth it for instrumentation.
- **Q5.** How does the register reach `iterate-plan`'s composed lens inputs, given composition is wholly runner-owned? **Recommendation: extend the runner's composition** to read `docs/risk-posture.md`'s `## Accepted risks` from the plan's repo root and append a register block, regenerating goldens. Reading a named file into the composed input is deterministic composition — squarely the runner's job ("the runner composes and invokes") — and keeps byte-determinism golden-pinned. Alternative: editor-side injection via the staged-note mechanism — no runner change, but it repurposes a channel built for manual-edit diffs and moves posture content outside the golden-pinned path, which is how composition drift starts.

## Out of scope

- **Fold-provenance stop-condition machinery (deliberately punted).** The 2026-08-21 doc-bot retro's item 3: making fold-provenance the loops' stop condition (decision cards with "delete the mechanism" / "split the plan" options). Punted by explicit decision because it carries real regression risk — fold-induced findings are sometimes load-bearing (the retro's pass-9/11/12 hit rates were 100%), so a naive stop condition would have halted reviews immediately before some of their best findings. Phase 0's instrumentation records the `introduced_by_pass` data now; the stop machinery is revisited only once several builds' worth of that data exists. Tracked as [issue #6](https://github.com/kaileconsulting/trinity-skills/issues/6); this plan's own review hand-pilots the rule (see Approach) to add one more observation.
- **Live validation of v2.3.0's posture machinery.** v2.3.0 shipped with its live-review acceptance open (2/6 met); the first real test remains doc-bot's next phase, which happens naturally and needs no phase here. Adding a validation phase would repeat the v2.3.0 pattern of acceptance criteria this repo can't close on its own schedule.
- **Generalizing lens-dropping.** Dropping the PM lens after APPROVE×3 is cited as precedent for question-freezing; turning it into a general selection rule is a separate, riskier change to fan-out semantics.
- **`iterate-review` v2 plan-binding** (`--plan` / `--phase`, plan-side Status automation) — existing deferral, unchanged by this plan.
- **Axis 1 (third-lens / dispute-corpus work)** — parked by standing decision; the dispute corpus sits at 4/6 and the build/no-build decision review triggers at 6, unchanged by this plan.
- **Retro-editing existing pass logs.** No backfilled `introduced_by_pass`, no re-review of shipped work; instrumentation is forward-only.
